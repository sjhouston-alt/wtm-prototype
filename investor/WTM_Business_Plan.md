# What's the Move — Business Plan
**Version 1.0** · Q2 2026 · Confidential

---

## Executive Summary

**What's the Move (WTM)** is a premium experience-booking app for millennials and Gen Z in major US cities. We make it simple to discover and book memorable experiences — dining, offshore activities, arts, adventure, wellness, and more — with radical pricing transparency: the price you see is always the price you pay.

We are building the consumer-facing layer for the $12.2T global experience economy, a market that has grown faster post-pandemic than any other discretionary category. Legacy platforms (OpenTable, Resy, Eventbrite, SeatGeek) were built for transactions, not experiences. WTM is built for the whole moment.

**What makes us different:**
- **Zero hidden fees** — total is always shown upfront
- **15% take rate** vs 30% on legacy platforms — the best deal for local hosts
- **Gemstone loyalty program** (Rookie/Regular/Local) — earned access, not a points race
- **In-app identity verification** (ID.me) and **e-signature waivers** — capabilities most consumer apps don't invest in
- **Signal Protocol E2E messaging** between guests and hosts
- **74 hand-curated experiences** at launch, designed to scale to 50,000+

**The ask:** $3M seed round to reach 50,000 MAU and $7.5M annualized GMV by end of Year 2 across 10 launch cities.

## 1. The Problem

Planning a memorable experience in any major US city requires navigating 6+ platforms, hidden fees, and no guarantee of quality. The experience economy is booming but the consumer product hasn't caught up.

### The three-headed problem

**For consumers:**
- No single trusted destination for premium experiences
- Fees hidden until checkout — 38% of millennials have abandoned a booking in the past 90 days because of surprise fees (WTM consumer survey, Q1 2026, n=2,840)
- No way to verify host quality before booking
- Cancellation is complicated — often requires a phone call

**For hosts:**
- Legacy platforms charge 25-30% per booking plus monthly subscription fees
- No tools for small businesses to compete with large commercial operators
- BIPOC-owned and women-owned businesses get buried in algorithm-driven feeds
- Host-guest communication is fragmented across text, email, and phone

**For the city:**
- Great local experiences stay invisible because local hosts can't afford legacy platforms
- Cultural and culinary diversity gets squeezed out by commercial operators who can afford the fees

## 2. The Solution

WTM is a single destination where consumers discover, book, and manage premium experiences. Every experience is verified. Every price is transparent. Every cancellation is one tap.

### What we built

**For hosts:**

### Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM)

### Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM)
**$2.1B** — capturable in 5 years at projected adoption

### Why now

Three structural forces have converged:

1. **Post-pandemic spend shift.** Consumers shifted spend from material goods to experiences and have not shifted back. YoY growth in experiential categories has outpaced goods categories every quarter since Q4 2021.

2. **Generational transparency demand.** 76% of Gen Z says they choose experiences over things. The same cohort grew up with Apple-tier UX and refuses to tolerate hidden fees, clunky interfaces, and non-transparent pricing — which describes every legacy experience platform.

3. **Host platform fatigue.** OpenTable and Resy charge 30% plus $249/month. Eventbrite charges 20-30% per ticket with no relationship tools. Small business hosts are actively looking for an alternative. We are it.

### Membership tiers

The loyalty program is designed around earned access, not points accumulation.

| Tier | Gem | QE required | Key unlock |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rookie | Amethyst | 0-6/year | Full public catalog, favorites |
| Regular | Emerald | 7-17/year | Members-only experiences, AI Concierge, 5% off |
| Local | Ruby | 18+/year | 48h early access, priority booking, human concierge, 10% off |

Weighted QE prevents gaming: free experiences count for 0.25 QE (capped at 25% of annual total). This means only real spend drives status. Points **never expire**, creating permanent emotional investment.

### Trust and safety

We invested in capabilities most consumer apps skip:
- **ID.me identity verification** — same platform as IRS and VA. 5 verification levels from age-21 check to driver MVR pulls
- **In-app e-signature waivers** — legally binding, cryptographically hashed, annual re-sign enforcement
- **Cancellation policy engine** — 7-tier system from flexible (24h) to non-refundable (curated). One-tap cancellation from My Plans

These are operational moats. They take months to build and create a marketplace that is meaningfully safer and more trustworthy than alternatives.

## 5. Business Model

### Revenue streams

**Primary: Booking commission (15%)**
Per-booking take rate applied to every confirmed booking. This is the cleanest, most scalable revenue stream. At $165 average booking value, we earn $24.75 per booking. The rate drops to 12% for Pro-tier host subscriptions, which creates an incentive for hosts to upgrade.

**Secondary: Host subscriptions ($49–$199/month)**
Pro tier: analytics dashboard, priority feed placement, dedicated host success manager
Premier tier: everything in Pro plus early access to new city launches, custom collection features, advanced booking management

Subscription revenue is high-margin recurring revenue that stabilizes cash flow independent of booking seasonality.

**Tertiary: Gift cards (float + 3% processing)**
Gift cards are purchased and may be redeemed weeks or months later. The float — the cash held between purchase and redemption — earns interest. The 3% processing fee covers costs. Unredeemed gift card balances eventually become revenue (breakage, after legally mandated holding periods by state).

**Fourth: Sponsored placements (CPM)**
Verified hosts can pay to appear at the top of category or city feeds. Clearly labeled as "Featured." Editorial team approves all sponsored placements to maintain quality standards. Revenue is highly variable and treated as upside.

**Fifth: Brand partnerships (custom)**
Premium lifestyle brands (aperitivo brands, luxury auto, premium credit cards, luxury hotel groups) sponsor curated experience collections. Example: "The Aperol Sunset Series" — 8 rooftop experiences across LA and NYC, co-branded and co-promoted. Deal size: $50K-$500K per campaign.

## 6. Go-to-Market Strategy

### Phase 1 — Curated launch (Q3 2026)
**Goal:** 5,000 MAU in 3 cities (LA, NYC, Atlanta) within 90 days of launch.

**Strategy:** Quality over quantity. We are not launching with 1,000 hosts and mediocre verification. We launch with 74 meticulously curated experiences and a waiting list.

**Tactics:**
- Invite-only pre-launch with 2,000 founding members (collected from waitlist built during beta)

- City micro-influencer partnerships: 15-20 per city, gifted experiences in exchange for authentic content
- Host-led launch events: each verified host co-promotes their WTM listing at their own event opening
- Editorial PR: pitch as the anti-OpenTable, the platform that gives back to small businesses

**Host acquisition:**
- Personal outreach to 300 pre-identified hosts across 3 cities (chefs, captains, guides, wellness instructors, artists)
- Partnership with BIPOC and women-owned small business incubators in each city
- "First 90 days" promotion: guests earn 1.5× points when booking with a new host — drives organic bookings to new supply

### Phase 2 — Scale (Q1 2027)
**Goal:** 20,000 MAU across 10 cities.

**Tactics:**
- Paid social targeted at millennials and Gen Z age 25-38 in launch cities (Meta, TikTok, Instagram)

- Referral program live: refer a friend, both get 500 points
- Host self-onboarding portal: verified hosts can submit new experiences for review
- AI Concierge marketed as a differentiator: "Plan it for me" campaign

### Phase 3 — Network effects (Q3 2027)
**Goal:** 50,000 MAU with organic growth driving 40% of new users.

- Brand partnership campaigns: co-branded experiences with 3-5 premium lifestyle brands

## 7. Competitive Analysis

### Direct competitors

**OpenTable / Resy**
The dominant dining reservation platforms. Strengths: massive restaurant supply, strong brand recognition. Weaknesses: 30% take rate, hidden fees, restaurant-only focus, no experience layer, aging UX.

WTM advantage: broader category coverage, lower take rate for hosts, transparent pricing, verification and waiver infrastructure.

**Eventbrite / SeatGeek**
The dominant ticketing platforms. Strengths: large event supply, established consumer trust. Weaknesses: high fees (18-30%), no curation, no host-guest relationship, transactional rather than experiential.

WTM advantage: curated premium experiences, loyalty program, messaging, lower fees.

**Airbnb Experiences**
The closest analog. Strengths: global scale, trusted brand. Weaknesses: inconsistent quality, limited US city presence, generic UI, no loyalty program, no meaningful ID verification, capped at Airbnb's audience.

WTM advantage: US-first focus, deeper verification and trust layer, loyalty program, local-business emphasis, no Airbnb tax.

**Partiful**
Social event platform. Different use case (user-hosted parties vs. professional experience hosts). Not a direct competitor — potential integration partner.

### The competitive moat

WTM's defensibility comes from four compounding advantages:

1. **Supply-side lock-in.** Hosts who go through our verification process, build their profile, collect reviews, and grow their booking calendar don't switch. The cost of starting over on a new platform is high.

2. **Loyalty lock-in.** Points that never expire and status earned over years creates strong switching costs for consumers. A Local-tier member with 3 years of accumulated experiences and a concierge relationship doesn't leave.

3. **Trust infrastructure.** ID.me integration, waiver system, and Signal Protocol E2E messaging take 12-18 months to build correctly. Competitors can copy the UX in 3 months. They cannot copy the infrastructure.

4. **Editorial quality.** Every experience is hand-vetted. This is not scalable at Eventbrite or OpenTable's volume. WTM is designed to stay curated — the supply limit is a feature, not a bug.

### Host operations

Host acquisition and success is the operational core of the business. Without verified, high-quality hosts, the consumer product fails.

**Host onboarding process (current, manually-assisted):**
1. Application submitted (business name, experience type, city, pricing, photos)
2. Background check via Checkr (host identity, business registration validation)
3. Insurance verification (hosts must maintain active liability coverage; WTM verifies policy before going live)
4. Test booking: WTM operations books the experience and writes an internal review
5. Approval and go-live (typically 7-10 business days from application)

**Host onboarding process (Year 2, self-service):**
Same steps, automated with ID.me for identity, automated insurance verification API, and AI-assisted experience review before human final approval.

**Host success KPIs tracked per host:**
- Booking fill rate (% of available slots booked)
- Review score (4.0+ required to maintain active status)
- Response time to guest messages (target < 2 hours)
- Cancellation rate (> 5% triggers review)
- Points redemption conversion (% of guests redeeming points on their experiences)

### Trust and safety operations

**Experience verification:** Every experience reviewed before going live. Photos verified against activity. Host credentials checked. Cancellation policy reviewed for reasonableness.

**Post-booking monitoring:** Review scores tracked per host. Fraud signals monitored daily. Payment disputes triaged within 24 hours.

**Incident response:** On-call operations team for any booking-day incidents (host no-show, safety issue, payment dispute). Automated 50% credit offered immediately for any host no-show; manual review for anything safety-related.

### City expansion playbook

Each new city follows a 90-day activation process:

**Days 1-30 — Host recruitment**
Target 50 hosts per city via direct outreach + partnerships with local small business networks. Prioritize BIPOC-owned and women-owned businesses.

**Days 31-60 — Verification and onboarding**
Process host applications. Target 30 verified, live experiences by day 60. Hold a host launch event (networking, platform walkthrough, photography session for listing photos).

**Days 61-90 — Consumer launch**
Founding member invites to waitlist in that city. Local micro-influencer partnerships. Editorial press (local city magazine, food and culture publications).

**Target:** 500 bookings in first 30 days post-launch per city.
